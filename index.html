Applies Throughout

Global Preferences

Wood Tone

Dark and mid-tone woods — walnut, mango wood, ebonised timber. Pale oak is the floor only: a light ground for dark furniture to sit against.

Lighting

Ambient over overhead, always. Lamps, Hue strips, candles. All bulbs 2700K maximum. Overhead switched off during evening and social use.

Hardware

Flat-plate matte black switch plates and sockets throughout (Forbes & Lomax or Corston). Radiators eventually in matt anthracite. Thermostat: Nest or Hive in black.

Reversibility

Freestanding and removable additions over permanent modifications. Exceptions only where function demands it.

Warmth Over Airiness

Darker walls over white. Richer textures over smooth. Containment over openness. When in doubt — choose the warmer option.

Japanese Sensibility

A quiet Tokyo interior, not a bright Stockholm apartment. The Japanese quality should be felt, not performed. No pastiche.

The Archway Threshold

The structural archway is given three interventions that honour the Japanese concept of transition between zones.

Colour

The soffit (underside) of the arch is painted in the feature wall colour — a warm-toned frame registered by the nervous system, not announced to the eye.

Sliding Walnut Door

Flush walnut veneer panel on a matte black top-mounted track. Aesthetic primary, practical secondary. Direct Doors or Vibrant Doors.

Trailing Plant

Pothos or string of hearts on a small wall-mounted bracket at the top left corner of the archway. Asymmetric, seasonal, alive — fukinsei in organic form.

Room Six

The Bathroom

Not structurally changed — tiles, suite, and layout are fixed. The work is entirely about what you bring into the room: warmth, texture, scent, discipline.

The warm wall colours in the corridor make the grey bathroom feel fresh and deliberate by contrast. A cool, clean counterpoint to the warm rooms around it.

Towels

Forest green — waffle-weave or Japanese-style cotton. Tekla, Kontex, Muji. Two or three sets. Green connects to Green Smoke and the kakejiku throughout the flat.

Bath Mat

Japanese sunoko — slatted teak or hinoki wood platform. Or simple dark linen bath mat. No fluffy carpet-style mats.

Scent

A small hinoki wood block or cedarwood disc on the vanity. Warmth releases a subtle woody scent — the classic Japanese bathhouse reference.

Surface Rule

Only visible on the basin: one hand soap, one hand lotion, a ceramic toothbrush holder. Everything else inside the mirrored cabinet.

Living with Intention

Seasonal Adaptation

A Japanese home is not static. Small, considered changes to textiles, scent, and objects that acknowledge the passage of time.

Winter

October — March

Heavier throws. Warmer scent — cedarwood, sandalwood, resinous incense. Candles lit earlier. Forest green throw on the bed. The flat turns inward.

Spring

April — May

Blossom stem in the Ombria vase. Lighter throws begin to appear. Windows cracked, sheers moving. Transitional scent: hinoki.

Summer

June — September

Lighter throws or none. Fresh scent — hinoki, yuzu, vetiver. Linen everything. Fewer cushions. The flat breathes.

Autumn

October

Dried grasses in the Ombria vase. Richer, deeper cushion tones. Candles return. The scent shifts to resin and wood.

A final principle

Every time something new is considered for this room, ask one question: does this add to the calm, or does it subtract from it? If the answer is uncertain, the answer is no.
